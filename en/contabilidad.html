INTERNATIONAL ACCOUNTING

Accounting for offshore and international structures

An international structure without organized accounting puts your regulatory compliance and your banking relationship at risk.

Many international structures operate without up-to-date accounting records, assuming that if they don't invoice locally there's nothing to report.

That assumption is the most common cause of banking blocks and regulatory observations affecting the entire structure.

Why it's critical for offshore compliance

Economic substance requirements and information-exchange standards like CRS require international structures to be able to demonstrate real activity and consistent accounting records. Without that evidence, banks and regulators tend to treat the structure as a risk signal, regardless of whether its operations are legitimate. This is not specific legal advice: each jurisdiction defines its own requirements.

ECONOMIC SUBSTANCE

Offshore jurisdictions require evidence of real activity to maintain tax benefits.

CRS AND REPORTING

Frequently asked questions

Do I need accounting if my offshore entity isn't actively operating?

Yes. Even without active operations, most jurisdictions require up-to-date accounting records to keep the structure in good legal standing.

How often is the balance sheet filed?

Generally on an annual basis, although the exact deadline depends on the jurisdiction and the type of structure you manage.
